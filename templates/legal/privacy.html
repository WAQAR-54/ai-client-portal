Scope

This policy applies to the web application, its sign-in and sign-up pages, the public pricing page, the shared invoice links we send to clients, and the emails the service sends. It applies to every kind of account: users, managers, department administrators and platform administrators. It does not apply to websites or services operated by other companies, including the AI providers described below, even where you reach them through the platform.

If your account was created for you by your organisation (for example by a department administrator), your organisation may have its own rules about your use of the service and may be an independent controller of some of your information. In that case, questions about what your organisation does with the information should be directed to your organisation.

Information We Collect

We collect only the information needed to run the service. It comes from three places: information you give us (for example when you sign up, type a prompt or upload a file), information created while you use the service (for example usage counts, security events and invoices), and information from other services you choose to connect (for example Google sign-in). The following sections describe each category in detail.

Account Information

When an account is created we store your email address (which is also your sign-in name), your password in hashed form only (we never store or can read your plain-text password), your role (user, manager, administrator or platform administrator), the department and team you belong to, and your display preferences (interface language and light/dark theme). You may add a first and last name to your profile. We also keep whether you have completed or skipped the first-login guided tour.

If you sign in with Google, we receive and store your Google account identifier, the email address and profile picture link Google provides, and the time the accounts were linked, so that the same person can be recognised on the next sign-in. We do not receive your Google password.

Your plan assignment (for example a trial or paid plan), its start and expiry dates, and whether you have cancelled it are stored against your account.

Authentication and Security Information

To protect accounts we process the following security information:

  • Two-step verification (MFA). Where two-step verification applies to your account (it is mandatory for administrator roles when the platform setting is on, and optional for other users who can turn it on in Settings), we generate a short-lived six-digit code, email it to you and keep it in your pre-sign-in session only until it is used or expires. The number of wrong attempts and resend requests is counted to prevent guessing.
  • Failed sign-in tracking. Repeated failed sign-ins for an account lock that account for a cooling-off period, and failed attempts are counted per address to slow automated attacks. Lock-out events are written to the security record together with the network address they came from.
  • Password reset. A password-reset request creates a single-use link that is emailed to the address on the account.
  • Session controls. A signed-in session ends after a period of inactivity. Signing in on a second browser or device signs the first one out, and a marker stored in the session is compared with the one stored on your account to do this.

Trusted Device / Session Information

If you use two-step verification, the browser you have just verified in becomes your one trusted device. We give that browser a random token in a cookie and store only a one-way hash of the token together with a short label (for example “Chrome on Windows”, derived only from the browser and operating-system names), when it was registered, when it was last used, when it expires and, if applicable, when it was revoked. We do not identify a device by fingerprinting, and the network address alone never makes a device trusted.

A trusted browser can sign in with your password without a new code until the trust expires or is revoked. Verifying on a different browser or computer replaces the previous trusted device, signs the earlier session out and sends you a “New trusted device” notice. In Settings you can see the current trusted device and use “Sign out all sessions” to revoke it and end every session.

Conversations and Prompts

We store the conversations you have with the service: the messages you send (prompts), the responses returned, conversation titles, timestamps, which model produced each response, the number of input and output tokens used and an estimate of their cost, and any feedback you give on a response. Conversations you pin, prompt templates you save and the order of your conversation list are stored so the interface behaves as you left it.

Conversations belong to the account that created them. Other users, including managers of your team, do not see the content of your conversations through the team and dashboard screens; those screens show counts and usage figures. Platform administrators operate the system and its stored records and may be able to access stored records when needed to operate, secure or support the service.

Uploaded Files and Documents

You can attach files to a message where your plan and role allow it. Attachments are checked against size and type limits and their content is inspected so that a file cannot pretend to be a different type or carry an executable. Accepted files are stored in the service’s file storage under your account, and the text or image content needed to answer your request is extracted and, when you ask the AI to work with the file, sent to the AI provider that handles the request (see AI Processing). Files you upload to prove payment of an invoice (a screenshot of a transfer, for example) are stored separately and are visible to the administrators who verify payments.

Please do not upload files you do not have the right to share, or files containing information you would not want processed by a third-party AI provider.

AI-Generated Outputs

Responses generated for you are stored as part of your conversation so you can read them again. Depending on your plan you can export a conversation or a single response to common document formats; those exports are produced on request from what is already stored. Output generated by an AI model may be inaccurate, and we treat it, for storage and privacy purposes, like the rest of your conversation content.

Project Information

Projects are personal folders you create to group your own conversations. We store the project name, when it was created and which conversations belong to it. Projects are private to your account; team and department views show only how many projects exist, not their names or contents. Deleting a project does not delete the conversations that were in it.

Billing and Payment Information

To issue invoices we store a billing profile for your organisation or account (company name, country, billing address, tax identifier and tax-exempt status, where you provide them) and the invoices themselves: the plan, amounts, tax, currency, issue and due dates, status and line items. When you tell us you have paid, we store the transaction reference and/or the proof-of-payment file you submit, who verified it and when. If you ask for a refund we store the request, your stated reason and the decision.

The application does not contain an online card-payment form and does not store payment card numbers. Payments are made outside the application and are confirmed by an administrator against the proof you submit. {% include "legal/_todo.html" with what="the payment methods accepted and the name of any payment processor or bank that handles funds" %}

Usage and Analytics Information

We measure how much of the service is used so that usage limits, plan allowances, budgets and invoices work: tokens used per day and month, estimated cost, number of requests, which features and models were used, and warnings when you approach a limit. Administrators see aggregated dashboards of this information for the people in their scope. The application does not include third-party advertising or behavioural-analytics trackers.

Technical and Device Information

When you connect we receive your network (IP) address and your browser’s user-agent string. We use them to operate and protect the service: to rate-limit abusive traffic, to record security events, to label a trusted device with browser and system names, and to make an initial guess of your interface language (a country-level lookup performed on our own server with an offline database, with no request to an outside service; the guess is only a starting point and any language you choose overrides it). Each request is also given a random request identifier that appears in our logs so that a problem can be traced.

Logs, Security and Audit Information

We keep several kinds of record:

  • Audit log. A record of important actions (sign-ins and lock-outs, role, plan and department changes, provider and model configuration, maintenance windows, billing and refund decisions and similar), with who did it, what it concerned and when. It is used for accountability and security investigations.
  • Email log. For emails the service sends (verification codes, notifications, invoices, reminders) we keep the recipient, subject, delivery status and whether the message was opened, which is detected by a tiny image in the email. Message bodies are not needed for this log.
  • Server and error logs. Technical logs with request identifiers. Where error monitoring is switched on, crash reports are sent to our error-monitoring provider with default personal data collection turned off and with credentials and secrets removed before sending.
  • Task and health records. Status of scheduled background jobs and system health checks, which contain no conversation content.

How We Use Information

We use information to: create and secure your account; sign you in and verify it is you; provide chat, comparison, document, project and other features you use; route each AI request to a model and provider; apply your plan’s allowances and limits; prepare invoices, verify payments and handle refunds; send service emails and in-app notifications; monitor performance, detect abuse and investigate incidents; comply with legal obligations; and improve the reliability of the service. AI Processing

The AI features are provided through third-party AI service providers. When you send a message and choose or are routed to a model, your prompt, the relevant earlier messages of the conversation that are needed to give context, any file content you attach, and the configuration needed to answer (such as instructions set by your organisation) are sent to the third-party AI provider that runs that model, so that the provider can process the request and return a response. Those providers process the content on their own systems, and the processing is governed by their own terms and privacy documentation as well as by this policy. For features that use several models at once (for example comparing two models) the same prompt is sent to each selected provider.

We do not promise that a provider does not keep, log or review requests, that requests never leave a particular country, or that a provider does not use content to improve its models: those practices depend on each provider and on the terms and settings applying to the connection, and they can change. The platform records, for each connected provider, whether the provider is understood to train on submitted data and administrators can restrict which providers and regions a department may use, but this is a configuration aid and not a guarantee. Data Security

We use measures appropriate to the service, including: password hashing; two-step verification and a limited number of trusted devices; account lock-out and rate limits against guessing; session time-outs and one active browser per account; role-based access controls that limit what each role can see and change; audit and security logging; encryption of provider credentials at rest and their exclusion from logs and error reports; encrypted connections (HTTPS) between your browser and the service; content checks on uploaded files; and regular backups where configured. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. Please keep your password and email account secure and tell us at once if you suspect misuse.
